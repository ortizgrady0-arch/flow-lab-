§01 — Why now

Three dated changes made a compliance-grade channel service sellable

None of them is a trend line. Two are supply-side facts with dates, the only kind of timing evidence that survived review; the third is a vendor survey of advisers and is directional only.

  1. Dec 2025

    YouTube gave every eligible channel native A/B testing ✓ verified

    Test & Compare reached all creators with Advanced Features: up to three title and thumbnail variants, winner decided on watch time, test closes inside two weeks. That turns packaging from taste into a measured result, which is what makes a $150–300 entry product provable to a sceptical firm.

  2. 16 Jul 2026

    YouTube named AI expert personas non-monetizable ✓ verified

    The clarified inauthentic-content policy lists three buckets that cannot monetize, the third being AI personas presenting as experts on health, legal, finance or politics. A licensed human on camera is the exact opposite pattern. Your competitors in the faceless-channel trade are on the wrong side of this line; you are on the right side of it by construction.

  3. Apr 2026

    Advisers want video and cannot produce it — vendor survey, directional

    Idea Decanter’s adviser survey: 88% think they should make video, 52% do, 83% say video helped increase assets under management, 80% give it only a few hours a month, 49% track nothing. Obstacles rank time 56%, hands-on shooting and editing expertise 52%, fresh ideas 38% — and cost last at 21%. Sample size unpublished, so treat as directional.

The one inference that shapes the whole offer

Cost is the smallest obstacle in that survey. Price is therefore not the objection you will face; trust and compliance are. So the thing you sell is not a lower rate than an agency. It is that nothing reaches their channel without their reviewer’s signature, and that the archive of approved versions supports their own books-and-records duty.

§03 — Who buys

Fee-only advisers first, estate firms second, accountants only as a test

The buyer pool is narrow rather than large, and that is fine: you need two clients. The constraint is finding and converting them, not market size.

Accountants are unreachable from January to April, so the sales window is October and November: twenty outbound touches then as a test, nothing more until data exists.

Exclude from the first cohort

  • Broker-dealer affiliated advisers. Principal pre-approval on retail communications reaching more than 25 retail investors in 30 days, which in practice is 14–16 approval items a month. Check the adviser registration record before spending a touch. A 2026 proposal would move this to risk-based supervision; revisit in 2027 if it is adopted.
  • Law firms in Florida and Texas. Per-ad filing fees and deadlines turn every deliverable into an administrative step with a cost.
  • Compliance-only accounting shops. Lead value is too low to carry the retainer.

The qualification rule

  • Registered adviser with no broker-dealer affiliation, or a law firm in a non-filing state.
  • An existing recording habit: a quarterly webinar, a podcast, past videos.
  • Fewer than one upload a month. They have the raw material and no throughput.
  • For an A/B pack, the last three uploads average 1,000+ views and the most recent is inside 90 days. A test needs roughly 1,000–5,000 fresh impressions per variant inside two weeks, so if nothing recent qualifies, run the test on their next upload rather than on an old video.
  • Advanced Features enabled on the channel, which Test & Compare requires. You cannot see this from outside, so confirm it on the call rather than while building the list. It sits in Studio under Settings, Channel, Feature eligibility, and is a five-minute verification on their side.

Two constraints that break the automation silently

  • Set the OAuth consent screen to “In production” before the client authorises. An app left in testing has every refresh token revoked after seven days, so a weekly Monday pull would fail every other week and the channel owner would have to re-consent each time. Channel manager permissions do not grant API access at all: only the owner can authorise it. While unverified you get an “unverified app” warning and a 100-user cap, both acceptable here. Keep the manual export from Studio as the permanent fallback so the memo never depends on the connection.
  • Descript meters media minutes with no rollover. Upload one clean master per recording, not every re-export, or you will buy top-ups you did not budget.

What machines cannot take over

  • Deciding which minutes become an episode.
  • Retention polish, which is most of the hours.
  • Proofreading statute numbers, form names and thresholds. An error here destroys credibility with this audience specifically.
  • The compliance pass and the approval loop.
  • Publishing, kept human on purpose.

  • Two clients at $800, $1,600 gross. 44–56 hours a month, 10–13 a week, $26–34 an hour.

  • Three clients at $1,000. $35–45 an hour but 15–19 hours a week, over budget.
  • Break-even: one A/B pack a month covers the tools; the first pack repays the whole month-0 spend.
§06 — First 90 days

Build a portfolio you do not have a client for, then send

Week one exists to make the first touch credible: two sample re-edits, a one-page site, ninety qualified firms, and a sending address that will not land in spam. Be honest about the load — this is a 25 to 30 hour setup week. On a 10 to 15 hour budget, spread it across two calendar weeks and expect the first touches to go out late in the second.

Decision gates

  1. Day 30
    100+ touches, 3+ replies, one pack sold or three calls booked

    Under three replies: rewrite the first line and move half the volume to connection notes. Zero replies after 100 touches and two rewrites: stop sending and get three warm introductions first.

  2. Day 60
    Three A/B packs sold to channels that clear the recency rule

    One or two sold: extend three weeks, cut the pack to $150, add a teardown video to every touch. Zero after 200 touches: kill, and move the hours to podcast clipping or a ghostwriting upsell.

  3. Day 90
    One Core retainer at $800+ signed, or a signed start date

    An adjust gate, not a kill gate. Also check the number that decides the economics: hours per client-month logged at or below 22, and approval cycle under three weeks. Continue past this only with two live proposals or a verbal yes.

  4. Day 120
    The kill gate

    No retainer at $800 or more after 300 personalised touches: demote this to a year-two upsell for ghostwriting clients and hand the hours over.

  5. Month 6
    Two clients, hours trending toward 16, one attributable booking each

    Two clients but hours over 20: stop selling, template the polish steps, then resume. One client and a churn: switch to the packaging-only micro-offer at $300–500 a month.

What the month looks like once a client is running. Memo and invoice on the 1st. Client records by the 5th. Transcript and rough cuts the 6th to 8th, polish and packaging the 9th to 12th, package to the reviewer by the 15th. On approval: one episode each Tuesday, Shorts Thursday and Saturday, a test running on every episode. Next-recording brief on the 25th.

§07 — Risk & kill

The five risks that should change how you work

Platform risk is genuinely low here, which is the point of the whole model. The real exposure is substitution, rework and the client’s own review burden.

Kill triggers

  1. Zero A/B packs sold after 200 touches, roughly eight weeks from the first send. One or two sold is the three-week extension in the day-60 gate, not a kill.
  2. No retainer at $800 or more by the end of month four, after 300 personalised touches.
  3. Two retainers running but per-client hours still above 22 at month six. The economics do not work at $800 and 22 hours.
  4. YouTube ships automatic clipping plus AI packaging for all creators and two prospects name it as the reason not to buy.

Pivots, in order

Cutdowns for LinkedIn sold to ghostwriting clients, reusing the same recording at about four hours a client-month. Then a packaging-and-compliance micro-offer at $300–500 for firms that already have an editor, with no editing hours at all. Then the podcast clipping retainer, which is the designated alternate. Last, white-label editing for one of the vertical specialist agencies: it trades margin for zero acquisition cost, and it is the right move if delivery quality is proven but outbound is not working.

§08 — Evidence

What this is built on, and what to check before you sign anything

This page is the third pass over the idea. A research dossier was written first, then two adversarial reviewers attacked it: one on demand, one on execution. Both weakened it; neither refuted it. Three judges then ranked it against nine other candidates. It placed first on durability and eighth of ten on automation, finishing inside a four-way tie at the top of the aggregate. The automation rank is the one that should change your behaviour: only 25 to 40% of production time can be automated, which is why the ceiling is two clients and why the hours check at day 90 is the real gate. Every number here is the post-review figure.

The honest caveat

No session in this research could fetch a primary page: the network proxy blocked every direct fetch. Every figure is relayed through search-result summaries of the linked sources, dated where the source is dated. Treat the whole page as “start here and validate before you sign anything”, not as a forecast.

Verify these five before a contract

  1. Tool prices at checkout. Descript, OpusClip, Canva, Claude and n8n all come from third-party 2026 guides, not vendor pages.
  2. Test & Compare mechanics: the impressions a variant needs and whether your prospect’s channel has Advanced Features enabled.
  3. Your state’s sales-tax treatment of editing services delivered as files. No data was found; over 30 states tax some digital products.
  4. The adviser survey’s sample size. It is a vendor survey with no published n, and it is the main demand evidence.
  5. Bar advertising rules in the state of any law firm you pitch, including whether educational content is exempt.
