## TL;DR

- Every major platform now has an explicit anti-"AI slop" monetization rule: YouTube (inauthentic content, Jul 2025; clarified Jul 2026), Meta (Jul 2025), TikTok Creator Rewards, Medium (May 2024), Etsy (Jun 2025), Deezer/Spotify (royalty exclusion), ACX (human-narration requirement). The hustle of "let AI mass-produce X and collect platform money" is dead on all of them.
- Hardest evidence: YouTube terminated 16 AI-slop channels in Jan 2026 (~4.7B views, 35M subs, ~$10M/yr ad revenue); Deezer gets ~75,000 fully-AI tracks/day (44% of uploads) and found up to 85% of their streams fraudulent; Upwork writing projects fell 32% YoY in 2025; Google search traffic to publishers fell ~1/3 in the year to Nov 2025.
- Price collapse is real in commodity gig work: Fiverr's $30 "200-word commercial read" middle market has "collapsed entirely," active buyers down double digits; Brookings finds text-heavy freelancers losing ~2%/month in new contracts. The premium tier (AI-related projects +44%/hr on Upwork) survives.
- Marketplace flooding (KDP, Etsy, Redbubble/Merch, stock sites, PromptBase) means search-driven discovery no longer works for undifferentiated AI output; PromptBase's top-40 sellers reportedly net only $200-400/month.
- The pattern of what survives: AI as the production layer under an original point of view, a narrow vertical, first-hand input, disclosure, and traffic you own (email, own store, direct clients) rather than platform search or platform payouts.
- For our operator (solo, 10-15 hrs/wk, <$500): avoid faceless-template YouTube, bulk KDP, Etsy prompt/AI-art dumps, generic Fiverr writing, AI streaming music, and "AI automation agency" as pitched. Treat those as "worked in 2023, policy-killed by 2026."
- Method caveat: 16 web searches completed before the session's search budget was exhausted; direct page fetches were blocked by the egress proxy, so figures are as reported by the linked sources (tiered A-D below) and not independently re-verified against primary pages. Six planned categories got no data (listed at the end).
- Cross-refs for future sessions: [[research/lenses/youtube-longform#Policy baseline (applies to every owned-channel candidate)]], [[research/saturated-overhyped#Cross-cutting patterns (the skeptic's rules)]], and per-hustle candidate dossiers under [[00-Index#Research]] should cite this note before shortlisting anything in these categories.

## Method and limitations

- Date of research: 2026-09-10. Operator profile assumed: one US person, side hustle, 10-15 hrs/wk, under $500 capital, comfortable with AI tools, no audience, no credential.
- 16 WebSearch queries succeeded (listed in Sources). The session's WebSearch budget (200/200) was then exhausted, and every WebFetch attempt (YouTube Help, Shutterstock, Medium Help, KDP, Etsy, Meta newsroom, Deezer newsroom, Tubefilter, Forbes, Press Gazette, Fast Company, Upwork, DuckDuckGo) was refused by the network egress proxy. Numbers below are therefore as reported in search-result summaries of the linked pages; none were re-verified against the primary page this session.
- Evidence tiers used below:
  - **A** = platform's own newsroom/help/policy page or regulator/official statistics (as summarized by search).
  - **B** = established trade or business press (Tubefilter, Digiday, Press Gazette, CNBC, Forbes, Fast Company, Search Engine Journal, AdExchanger) or a named research body (Brookings, Ramp, CISAC, Upwork Research).
  - **C** = vendor blogs, SEO content, tool-company "guides," Medium posts, Reddit. Directionally useful, numerically unreliable.
  - **D** = promotional guru claims ("$2K-$20K/month"). Cited only as the claim being debunked.
- "No data found" is written where I could not source a number.

## The hustles

### 1. Faceless "YouTube automation" channels (AI script + AI voice + stock/AI visuals)

**As promoted:** Spin up 5-10 faceless channels with ChatGPT scripts, ElevenLabs narration, Pictory/InVideo visuals; monetize through AdSense; "$10K/month passive."

**Why it fails now:** YouTube rewrote the Partner Program rule on 15 Jul 2025, renaming "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" and defining it as mass-produced, repetitive, or low-effort video with minimal human creativity. Enforcement is at channel level, and reporting says it is retroactive across all uploads. In Jan 2026 YouTube made its largest single move: 16 channels terminated with ~4.7B lifetime views and ~35M subscribers, an estimated ~$10M/yr of ad revenue erased; named channels included CuentosFacianantes (5.95M subs, AI Dragon Ball narratives that differed only in names), Imperiodejesus (5.87M, multiple AI-narrated biblical stories per day), Super Cat League (4.21M, AI animal content). On 13 Jul 2026 YouTube clarified further that "generic or repetitive content," content that is "unsatisfying or off-putting," and content featuring fake AI "experts" cannot be monetized. The classic templates (Reddit-story narration with the same voice/music/visuals, motivational-quote compilations over stock B-roll) are the exact patterns cited. Note the conflict of interest: many "how to avoid demonetization" guides are published by the faceless-video tool vendors themselves (Fliki, AITuber, Moshion, MilX).

**Evidence:**
- Policy rename and definition, 15 Jul 2025 - tier B (AlternativeTo news; Medium/Starcevic; ytgrowth).
- Jan 2026 termination wave: 16 channels / 4.7B views / 35M subs / ~$10M annual ad revenue; named channels - tier B/C (OutlierKit resources; Medium/Starcevic; bottlerocketcontent). Original YouTube statement not fetched.
- 13 Jul 2026 policy clarification ("generic or repetitive," "fake AI experts") - tier B (Tubefilter).
- "Thousands of AI channels suspended" - tier C (MilX).

**What still works:** Faceless is not banned; templated is. Channels survive when there is an original script from real research, a consistent narrator persona and editorial point of view, curation or analysis that transforms sources, and a visual style that is not interchangeable. AI is acceptable as the research assistant, editor, thumbnail generator, and even the voice, provided the content is transformative. Treat AdSense as secondary and plan sponsorship/affiliate/product revenue, because a single policy strike zeroes AdSense. For a 10-15 hr/wk operator: one channel, one niche, weekly cadence, not ten channels daily. See [[research/candidates/workflow-tutorial-channel]].

### 2. Amazon KDP AI-generated ebooks and low-content books

**As promoted:** Generate 50 ebooks/journals/coloring books with ChatGPT and Midjourney, upload in bulk, collect royalties.

**Why it fails now:** KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated text, images, and translations, and reporting through 2025-2026 describes enforcement moving from removing individual books to account-level suspension for undisclosed AI content. Low-content books are still permitted, but Amazon is removing duplicates, mass-generated uploads, misleading metadata, and bulk AI interiors lacking meaningful differentiation; there is no published numeric cap, but large volumes of similar books raise scrutiny. (A widely reported 2023 daily-title cap was not re-verified this session.) Separately, the low-content niche (journals, planners) was already saturated before AI; AI removed the last barrier to entry.

**Evidence:**
- Disclosure requirement and account-level enforcement - tier C (KDP Builder 2026; univers.studio; inkfluenceai; AI Policy Desk, whose headline references a tripling in AI-generated submissions). KDP help page not fetched.
- Low-content policy: allowed but duplicates/mass uploads removed; no stated limit - tier C (pubnook 2026).
- Sales numbers for AI books: no data found.

**What still works:** Disclosed, human-edited, niche nonfiction where you contribute real expertise, original data, interviews, or a genuinely new structure; one series with reader retention rather than 50 orphans; pairing with Amazon's own Virtual Voice audiobook (see #11) and a small email list. Use AI for outlining, drafting, and editing, not for the whole interior. See [[research/lenses/digital-products]].

### 3. Etsy AI art, AI printables, and AI prompt bundles

**As promoted:** Midjourney wall art, "AI-generated planner" PDFs, and packs of prompts sold as digital downloads with zero fulfilment.

**Why it fails now:** In June 2025 Etsy updated its Creativity Standards. Items marked "Made by" the seller must now be created from scratch by that seller; the prior exception for templated designs (Canva templates, purchased design bundles, licensed 3D models) is gone, and the change was applied retroactively with no grace period. AI-generated listings must be labeled "Designed by" rather than "I made it," and must carry an explicit AI disclosure in the description. AI prompt bundles are banned outright. One guide reports ~12,000 listings removed in a single quarter for mislabeling (unverified). Beyond policy, Etsy search is flooded with near-identical AI art, so undifferentiated listings get no discovery.

**Evidence:**
- June 2025 Creativity Standards changes, retroactive - tier C (bulkmockup; listybox; iscompliant). Etsy legal page not fetched.
- "Designed by" requirement, AI disclosure, prompt bundles banned - tier C (bulkmockup; iscompliant; printablekit).
- ~12,000 listings removed in one quarter - tier C, unverified.
- Sales data for AI art sellers: no data found.

**What still works:** "Designed by" listings with honest disclosure where AI is the base and you do real design work on top (composition, typography, editing); personalization and custom orders (buyer-specific input is hard to flood); functional printables for a specific profession or life situation rather than generic planners; selling from your own store/Shopify with Pinterest or short-form traffic so one policy change cannot delete the business. See [[research/candidates/personalized-ai-kids-books]].

### 4. Fiverr commodity AI gigs (writing, voiceover, logo, "I'll use ChatGPT for you")

**As promoted:** List $5-30 gigs, use AI to fulfil in minutes, scale on volume.

**Why it fails now:** The buyer of a $30 commodity deliverable now uses ChatGPT directly. Fast Company's coverage of Fiverr's Q2 2026 results says the middle of the market, "where buyers used to pay $30 for a 200-word commercial read, has collapsed entirely," and that Fiverr lost about 14% of active buyers last year (to ~3.1M). Another summary (Metaintro) puts the active-buyer decline at 21.9% as AI absorbed low-value gig work (different period; figures not reconciled). Fiverr itself is repositioning toward high-end work. Any gig whose value is "I know how to prompt" has zero moat.

**Evidence:**
- Active buyers down ~14% to ~3.1M; middle market collapsed; Fiverr stock collapse framed as AI decimating gig economy - tier B (Fast Company, 2026 Q2 earnings coverage).
- Active buyers down 21.9% - tier C (Metaintro); discrepancy with above noted.
- Fiverr shifting to high-end work, 2025 results/2026 outlook - tier C (HR Tech Feed).
- "AI cut freelance rates 30%"; early adapters earn 40-60% more per hour - tier C (Winvesta blog), directional only.

**What still works:** Pick one industry and go deep (the writers still earning on Fiverr "picked one industry and went deep"); sell outcomes and judgment (e.g., a conversion-tested landing page for a specific vertical) with AI as the invisible backend; productize higher-ticket packages and move repeat clients off-platform. See [[research/candidates/youtube-manager-expert-firms]].

### 5. Upwork generalist AI-assisted writing and copyediting

**As promoted:** Bid on writing jobs, use Claude/ChatGPT to deliver 5x faster, undercut on price.

**Why it fails now:** Writing projects on Upwork declined 32% year over year in 2025, the largest drop of any category; entry-level project availability fell below 9% from 15% the prior year. A Brookings analysis found freelancers in text-heavy services (copyediting, proofreading) losing roughly 2% of new contracts per month and ~5% of monthly on-platform earnings. Ramp's Feb 2026 "Payrolls to Prompts" study found more than half of businesses that spent on freelance platforms in 2022 had stopped entirely by 2025. Newcomers without reviews are competing for a shrinking entry tier.

**Evidence:**
- Upwork writing projects -32% YoY 2025; entry-level <9% from 15% - tier B (Mediabistro summarizing Upwork data).
- Brookings: ~2%/month decline in new contracts, ~5% earnings decline for text-heavy freelancers - tier B (via Mediabistro).
- Ramp Feb 2026: >50% of 2022 freelance-platform spenders stopped by 2025 - tier B (via Mediabistro).
- AI-related projects earn 44% more per hour than non-AI projects; content writing in top 10 in-demand AI-related skills Sept 2025 - tier B (Upwork research via Mediabistro/Upwork blog).

**What still works:** Position as the "AI-related" freelancer, not the writer: content systems, AI editing/QA for regulated or technical domains, prompt-and-workflow builds, documentation for AI products. Domain expertise plus AI fluency is the only pricing power left in this category. See [[research/lenses/freelance-marketplaces]].

### 6. AI-written niche / affiliate websites for Google traffic

**As promoted:** Generate 500 articles with an AI writer, rank on Google, earn Mediavine/Amazon affiliate income.

**Why it fails now:** The traffic source is disappearing. Press Gazette reports global publisher traffic from Google fell by roughly a third in the year to November 2025; Digiday links AI Overviews to a ~25% drop in publisher referral traffic; the median year-over-year decline is about -10% overall and -14% for non-news brands, with small independent publishers reported at the high end (60-75%+ in some cases). Even large brands are hit (Business Insider organic search -55% Apr 2022 to Apr 2025; Stereogum lost ~70% of ad revenue in a year). Zero-click AI answers are expected to become default for informational and how-to queries through 2026, which is exactly what AI-written affiliate sites target. AI writers also produce the "scaled content" Google's spam policies name.

**Evidence:**
- Google traffic to publishers down ~1/3 in year to Nov 2025 - tier B (Press Gazette, 2026).
- AI Overviews linked to ~25% drop in referral traffic - tier B (Digiday).
- Median -10%/-7% news/-14% non-news; most sites lost 1-25% - tier B (Search Engine Journal and coverage).
- Small publishers 60-75%+; CTR down up to 89% on some queries - tier C (magazinecoalition, psyke).
- Business Insider -55%; Stereogum -70% ad revenue - tier B (AdExchanger / press coverage).

**What still works:** Sites with first-hand testing, original data, calculators/tools, and a product to sell (not display ads); traffic from YouTube, Pinterest, newsletters, and communities rather than Google; being the cited source inside AI answers for a narrow topic. Affiliate-only AI content sites should be treated as end-of-life. See [[research/lenses/owned-audience-seo]].

### 7. Medium Partner Program with AI-generated stories

**As promoted:** Publish 3 AI articles a day on Medium, earn from member reading time.

**Why it fails now:** Since 1 May 2024 Medium bars AI-generated writing (disclosed or not) from the paywall; "AI-generated" means most of the content was produced by an AI tool with little editing, fact-checking, or change. Violations can remove stories from the paywall or revoke Partner Program enrollment. Since 1 Apr 2025, stories primarily about Medium or the Partner Program are also ineligible (killing the "how I make money on Medium" loop). Medium payouts were never large for unknown writers.

**Evidence:**
- May 2024 AI paywall ban and enforcement - tier A/B (Medium Help Center terms as summarized; TechTimes; AlternativeTo; Hacker News thread).
- April 2025 rule on Medium-about-Medium stories - tier C (Medium posts summarizing the policy).
- Earnings data: no data found.

**What still works:** Human-written, AI-assisted (outline, edit, fact-check, grammar) pieces are explicitly allowed. Use Medium as distribution to an email list or product, not as the income line.

### 8. AI-generated stock images on stock marketplaces

**As promoted:** Generate thousands of Midjourney images, upload to Adobe Stock/Shutterstock, earn passive royalties.

**Why it fails now:** Shutterstock does not accept AI-generated content from contributors for licensing, citing the inability to assign IP ownership. Adobe Stock accepts labeled AI content but Adobe's own AI (Firefly) is cannibalizing the stock library "more quickly than planned for": management reported the traditional stock business (about a $450M revenue line) saw a steeper decline than expected while Firefly ARR passed $250M. Buyers now generate their own images; a marketplace of millions of near-identical AI images has collapsing per-asset value.

**Evidence:**
- Shutterstock AI submission ban and IP rationale - tier A (Shutterstock Contributor FAQ, as summarized).
- Adobe stock decline, ~$450M line, Firefly ARR >$250M growing 75% QoQ - tier B (Yahoo Finance/Motley Fool/Forbes coverage of Adobe earnings, 2026).
- Contributor earnings per asset: no data found.

**What still works:** Bespoke AI imagery for specific clients (product mockups, brand-consistent social visuals) sold as a service; niche asset bundles with editing and licensing clarity sold from your own store; Adobe Stock only as a long-tail extra with correct labeling, never the plan.

### 9. Print-on-demand with AI designs (Redbubble, Merch by Amazon, Etsy POD)

**As promoted:** Upload 1,000 AI-generated designs across Redbubble/Merch/Etsy; let the marketplace sell.

**Why it fails now:** Redbubble reportedly has a flood of AI designs burying original work, plus declining royalty rates and algorithm changes; one POD vendor says many sellers report earning 50-70% less than in 2020-2022, with generic humor tees "flatlining." Redbubble allows AI-assisted designs but removes anything echoing protected characters or brands regardless of origin; Amazon Merch places all rights responsibility on the creator. Etsy's June 2025 rules (see #3) remove the templated-design exception POD sellers relied on. Marketplace search is the discovery channel, and it is precisely what flooding breaks.

**Evidence:**
- AI flood, artists leaving, royalty declines; 50-70% lower earnings - tier C (Merch Titans, vendor blog).
- Redbubble AI-permitted-but-IP-policed; Amazon rights responsibility - tier C (merchbanao, pixelforge).
- Etsy June 2025 rules - see #3.

**What still works:** Sharp niche plus personalization, trend responsiveness, hand-refined AI concepts, and your own audience/store as the traffic source rather than marketplace search. See [[research/candidates/personalized-ai-kids-books]].

### 10. AI-generated music uploaded to Spotify/Deezer for streaming royalties

**As promoted:** Generate hundreds of Suno/Udio tracks, distribute via DistroKid, earn per-stream royalties on autopilot.

**Why it fails now:** Deezer reports ~75,000 fully AI-generated tracks per day, about 44% of new uploads (>2M/month), and found that up to 85% of streams on fully AI tracks in 2025 were fraudulent; it excludes manipulated streams from royalties and lets users filter AI music. Spotify removed 75M spammy tracks in 12 months and pays no royalties until a track passes 1,000 streams in 12 months. Legit AI tracks compete with millions of others; the only way people were "earning" was stream-farming, which is now detected and demonetized. CISAC/PMP project ~25% of creator revenues (up to about EUR 4B) at risk by 2028.

**Evidence:**
- 75,000 AI tracks/day, 44% of uploads, 85% fraud, royalty exclusion - tier A (Deezer Newsroom, Apr 2026, as summarized; Chartlex/pophits coverage).
- Spotify 75M spam tracks removed, 1,000-stream threshold - tier B (Forbes, May 2026).
- CISAC/PMP ~25% / EUR 4B by 2028 - tier B (via iMusician/Forbes).

**What still works:** Do not chase streaming royalties. Use AI music as an input: background beds for your own videos, custom jingles/intros for small businesses or podcasters sold directly, sync-style licensing to creators with clear terms. Check each library's AI policy first; many royalty-free libraries also restrict fully AI tracks.

### 11. AI-narrated audiobooks and AI voiceover gigs

**As promoted:** Turn KDP books into audiobooks with an AI voice via ACX; sell AI voiceovers on Fiverr.

**Why it fails now:** ACX's direct submission requirements (as of 15 Apr 2026) say audiobooks must be human-narrated unless authorized and list unauthorized text-to-speech/AI recordings as prohibited. AI narration reaches Audible only through Amazon's own Virtual Voice program or via aggregators (Spotify-Findaway/INaudio, Author's Republic). ACX's royalty model changed in May 2026 to 50% of "Member Value" exclusive / 30% non-exclusive, and reporting says a non-exclusive AI voice can drop you to the 30% tier. On the gig side, the $30 short commercial read that made "AI voiceover" a Fiverr hustle is the exact market Fast Company describes as having "collapsed entirely."

**Evidence:**
- ACX human-narration requirement, AI prohibited for direct submission, Apr 2026 - tier B/C (Audie, storyvox summaries of ACX requirements).
- Virtual Voice and aggregator pathway - tier B/C (Narration Box, storyvox).
- May 2026 royalty model 50%/30% - tier C (Chapter blog, alpacaauthors).
- Voiceover middle-market collapse - tier B (Fast Company, see #4).

**What still works:** Virtual Voice on your own KDP titles (Amazon's sanctioned path); human narration with AI-assisted editing/mastering if you have a good voice; emotive or character work AI still does badly; brand-voice work for repeat clients at higher tickets.

### 12. Short-form AI slop on TikTok, Reels, and Facebook (Creator Rewards / Meta bonuses)

**As promoted:** Post AI-avatar or AI-voiceover videos 10x/day, get paid via TikTok Creator Rewards, Facebook bonuses, or Shorts.

**Why it fails now:** TikTok's Creator Rewards Program excludes unoriginal content (copied or minimally edited) and treats fully synthetic, low-effort, or undisclosed AI content as downranked or ineligible; AI-generated visuals and audio must be labeled and are subject to suppression. TikTok has auto-detected AI via C2PA credentials since Jan 2025. Meta announced on 14 Jul 2025 that it actioned ~500,000 accounts in H1 2025 for spammy behavior (reduced distribution through blocked monetization) and removed ~10M impersonator profiles; Facebook penalizes reused content without added commentary. YouTube Shorts fall under the inauthentic-content policy in #1.

**Evidence:**
- TikTok CRP originality rules - tier A (TikTok Creator Academy) with interpretation - tier C (Storrito, monetizednow, Kompozy).
- C2PA auto-labeling Jan 2025 - tier C (Storrito).
- Meta: 500,000 accounts actioned, 10M profiles removed, Jul 2025 - tier A/B (Meta newsroom as reported by CNBC, Forbes, Tubefilter).

**What still works:** AI as production layer (script, edit, B-roll, captions) under original on-camera or original-voice content with disclosure; TikTok Shop affiliate with real product handling and demonstration (an anecdotal "70/30 human/AI" rule from a TikTok Shop vendor is tier C); reaction/commentary that adds a real perspective.

### 13. Selling AI prompts (PromptBase, Etsy, Gumroad)

**As promoted:** "Selling AI prompts is a $2.5B market"; "I made $2,000 last month selling prompts"; "$2K-$20K/month."

**Why it fails now:** Prompts are trivially copied, most models now write good prompts, and marketplaces are flooded. A critical analysis of PromptBase reports the platform's top-40 sellers clearing only $200-400/month after fees and calls the $4.99 single prompt "a dead format." Etsy banned AI prompt bundles outright (see #3). The "$2.5B market" number comes from a report about the whole prompt-engineering industry, not marketplace payouts to individuals.

**Evidence:**
- Top-40 PromptBase sellers $200-400/month after fees; $4.99 prompt dead - tier C (digiko analysis).
- "500,000 prompts sold on PromptBase in 2025"; "$100-2,000/month average" - tier C, unverified (softhubtools).
- "$2K-$20K/month"; "$2,000 last month" - tier D (NeuraPulse; AiTechWorlds).
- Etsy prompt-bundle ban - tier C (see #3).

**What still works:** Prompts packaged inside a workflow, template, or service for a specific job (e.g., a real-estate listing system with prompts plus SOPs), sold through your own channel or as a subscription; never as standalone $5 listings.

### 14. "AI automation agency" (AAA) / chatbot agency as pitched by gurus

**As promoted:** Learn n8n/Make, cold-DM 100 businesses a day offering AI chatbots or automations, close $2-5K retainers, no experience needed.

**Why it fails now:** The pitch is saturated even if delivery is not: every guru course produces hundreds of identical "AI automation agencies" cold-messaging the same SMBs. Sources note only ~18% of US firms had adopted AI by end-2025 and ~14% of SMBs have it fully embedded, so buyers are early, skeptical, and expensive to acquire; the most common failure is not technical but post-launch (no one maintains the client relationship or the brittle automation). At 10-15 hrs/wk with no network, sales volume is the constraint, and generic chatbots are now bundled free into the tools SMBs already pay for.

**Evidence:**
- Generic pitch saturated / content saturation vs delivery saturation; 18% US firms, 14% SMBs; post-launch failure mode - tier C (Layer3labs, Ciela guides; underlying adoption figures likely from Census Bureau/industry surveys, not verified).
- Demand-side support: AI-related projects +44%/hr on Upwork - tier B (see #5).
- Agency revenue/failure statistics: no data found.

**What still works:** One vertical, one measurable outcome, one delivery template (e.g., missed-call text-back and appointment reminders for one type of local business), sold as a maintained retainer to a handful of clients found through local referrals. Two or three clients is the realistic ceiling at this time budget. See [[research/candidates/ai-front-desk-home-services]].

## Cross-cutting patterns (the skeptic's rules)

1. **Platform payouts are the first thing to die.** AdSense, Creator Rewards, Medium, streaming royalties, marketplace search: every one has an anti-slop rule now. Any plan whose income is a platform payout for volume is policy-exposed.
2. **Flooding kills search discovery.** KDP, Etsy, Redbubble, stock sites, PromptBase, Google itself. If the plan needs "get found in search," it needs a differentiator flooding cannot copy: personalization, first-hand data, a named persona, a vertical.
3. **The middle price tier is gone; the top and the free tier remain.** Fiverr's $30 read and the $200 blog post are gone; the $2K vertical system and the free ChatGPT answer remain.
4. **Disclosure is now mandatory almost everywhere** (KDP, Etsy, TikTok, YouTube labels). Hiding AI use is an account-level risk, not a listing-level one.
5. **What survives is AI-under-human, not human-under-AI**: original judgment on top, automation underneath, and traffic or clients you own.
