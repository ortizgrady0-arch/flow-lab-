## TL;DR

- Overall: **weakened**. The market-size numbers may be right, but the dossier's "who pays and how much" chain does not survive its own arithmetic: applying its figures (42% affiliate share of $15.1B, 13% average commission, ~945K US creators with any sale, top 0.5% taking 38%) gives a mean commission pool of roughly **$45/month per non-elite affiliate**, not the $200-1,500/month nano tier it quotes.
- The creator-earnings statistics the dossier leans on are mutually inconsistent: "top 1% average ~$600K GMV" and "top 5% average ~$80K GMV" cannot both be true for any population size (0.01N x 600K > 0.05N x 80K). Treat wecantrack/InfluencerFee tier data as unreliable, not merely anecdotal.
- The base case ($400/month at month 6) rests on two unsourced assumptions (2,000 average views per post on a ~5K-follower account; 2% of viewers tapping the product card) and on a targeted-plan invite that normally follows demonstrated GMV. Corrected base: **$50-150/month**; the dossier's "conservative" row is the modal outcome by its own 59%-churn claim.
- AI is not a differentiator here: scripting, hooks, product scoring and captions are sold to every affiliate (Kalodata, EchoTik, FastMoss, TikTok Symphony). The only scarce input is the operator's face and hands, which is the un-automatable 4-5.5 h/week. The candidate is a creator job with AI clerical help, not an AI-leveraged business.
- Pricing pressure is one-directional: referral fee 6% -> 8% (2026-08-04; 9.5-11% in beauty/electronics sub-categories) lands on the same sellers who set open-plan commissions, and "more than half of 803.5K US stores had zero sales" means the buyers of affiliate content are mostly broke. The dossier admits no evidence of falling open-plan rates; there is equally none of them holding.
- "Beauty is #1 category, so it is the right lane" is weakened: beauty carries the highest referral-fee tier, the tightest efficacy-claim policing (CHR points, FTC), and the densest competition; kitchen/cleaning under $40 is the safer of the three.
- Verification status: WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200) before this pass and all 22 WebFetch attempts (Momentum Works, TikTok Newsroom, Seller University, FTC, Hamster Garage, Dashboardly, Affiverse, Delzonic, Reddit x3, archive.org, Wikipedia, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Google Trends, Marketplace Pulse, Tubefilter, Business Insider) were egress-blocked. **No new external data was obtained**; every verdict below is either an internal-consistency test or "no data found". Re-run with web access before a go/no-go.
- Corrected scores: demand 8 -> **6** (large pool, negligible per-entrant share), economics 4 -> **3**. Keep it as the operator's social line only, which is what the dossier already concludes.

## Method and limits

Date: 2026-09-10. Task: assume the demand case is overstated and try to refute it. The dossier ([[research/candidates/tiktok-shop-affiliate-demos]]) itself reports that all its figures come from search-result summaries, none fetched. This pass could not improve on that: the session had no search budget left and every fetch was blocked. What I could do:

1. Check the dossier's numbers against each other (they are all in one place, so contradictions are testable).
2. Check them against the other vault notes written the same day: [[research/lenses/shortform-social]], [[research/lenses/commerce-arbitrage]], [[research/saturated-overhyped]], [[research/shortlist]].
3. Write "no data found" wherever a refutation would need an outside source (Reddit post-mortems, Google Trends, 2026 run-rate GMV, category return rates, post-August open-plan commission trend).

Evidence labels: **V** platform/primary (as relayed by search, never fetched), **R** analytics/agency secondary, **A** anecdotal, **I** internal arithmetic on the dossier's own figures. Per the brief, any claim without support either way is marked weakened.

## Claim-by-claim

### D1. US TikTok Shop GMV $15.1B in 2025, +68% YoY (V, Momentum Works)

- Verdict: **weakened** (unverified, single source, relayed secondhand).
- Evidence: the dossier's own table lists a competing vendor figure ($15.82B, +108%, Hamster Garage). Two figures 5% apart on level but 40 points apart on growth means at least one 2024 base is wrong; nobody in the vault has read the Momentum Works report. Growth-rate uncertainty matters because the "still growing" framing rests on it. No 2026 year-to-date run-rate data found (fetch blocked).
- Note: GMV is seller revenue, not affiliate income; even if exact it says nothing about the per-creator share. See D4.

### D2. Affiliate content drives ~42% of US GMV (R, Hamster Garage)

- Verdict: **weakened**.
- Evidence: single vendor blog; not in the Momentum Works channel split the dossier quotes (video 50%, shop tab 36%, live 14%). 42% affiliate out of 64% content-driven GMV would mean affiliates account for two-thirds of all video+live sales, with the remaining third from sellers' own accounts. Plausible, unverifiable. Hamster Garage is an affiliate-management agency with a commercial reason to size the channel generously.

### D3. Closed-loop conversion ~4.7%, affiliate-link engagement 5.2% (R, Hamster Garage)

- Verdict: **weakened**.
- Evidence: no methodology, no population, no date range in any vault note. The dossier applies 4.7% to product-page clicks, then needs a second, entirely unsourced number (2% of viewers tap the product card in the base case, 1.5% conservative) to reach orders. The base case therefore rests on a 0.094% view-to-order rate (2% x 4.7%) that no source supports. no data found for a view-to-order benchmark.

### D4. Nano tier (1-10K followers) earns $200-1,500/month (A, Branvas)

- Verdict: **refuted** as a typical figure; **weakened** as a ceiling.
- Evidence (I): combine the dossier's own inputs. Affiliate GMV = 42% x $15.1B = $6.34B/yr. At the 13.02% average commission the total US affiliate commission pool is ~$826M/yr. Top 0.5% of ~945K creators with any sale (~4,700 accounts) take 38% = $314M (~$66K each). The other 99.5% (~940K) share $512M = ~$545/yr = **~$45/month mean**, before clawbacks, and the median is well below the mean in any power-law distribution. A $200/month floor for the nano tier is inconsistent with the pool size unless the 945K figure overstates active creators by 4-5x, in which case the dossier's competition count is wrong instead. Either way one of the numbers has to go.
- Corrected: typical nano affiliate commission is on the order of tens of dollars per month; $200-1,500 is a top-decile band. no data found on the median.

### D5. Top 1% average ~$600K annual GMV; top 5% ~$80K GMV (R/A, wecantrack/InfluencerFee)

- Verdict: **refuted** (internally impossible).
- Evidence (I): for any creator count N, the top 1% alone would hold 0.01N x $600K = 6,000N in GMV, while the top 5% (which contains the top 1%) would hold only 0.05N x $80K = 4,000N. A superset cannot hold less than its subset. At least one figure is wrong, and neither should be used. Also (I): with N = 945K, the top 1% at $600K would be $5.67B, i.e. 89% of the entire $6.34B affiliate GMV, which contradicts "top 0.5% drive 38%".
- Corrected: no data found; strike both figures from the dossier and from [[research/lenses/shortform-social]].

### D6. Top 0.5% drive 38% of affiliate GMV; 59% churn by month 12; survivors' median up 6x (R, Dashboardly/wecantrack)

- Verdict: **weakened** (unverified, primary dataset unnamed).
- Evidence: directionally consistent with D4 and with the 15.4M registered vs ~945K earning gap (6% of registrants make any sale). The "survivors' median up 6x" line is survivorship bias by construction and should not be read as an expected trajectory. Note the dossier uses the 59% churn as evidence its conservative case is modal and still headlines the base case; those are incompatible.

### D7. Base case $400/month at month 6; upside $1,500+ (I, dossier model)

- Verdict: **weakened**; corrected base **$50-150/month**.
- Evidence (I): base requires (a) 2,000 average views on 60 posts/month from an account that only just exited the Pilot Program at 5,000 followers (no source for that view level; the dossier's own conservative case uses 800), (b) 2% product-card CTR (no source), (c) 4.7% conversion (D3), (d) a targeted plan at 20% by month 6, which sellers normally extend after visible GMV. Removing (d) and using 1,200 views / 1.5% CTR / 4.7% / 15% clawback / $3.75 per order gives ~$160 gross, ~$135 net; using the conservative view level gives ~$50. Implied hourly at 12 h/week: **$1-3/h**, not $8.
- Upside: a 300-500K-view clip in Q4 is possible but is a lottery ticket, not a plan; no base rate found for nano accounts.

### D8. Time to first deposit 60-100 days (dossier)

- Verdict: **weakened**; corrected **90-150 days**, no data on median.
- Evidence: the 1,000-follower gate has no verified time-to-reach ("anecdotally 3-8 weeks", dossier); the Pilot Program is a fixed 30 days; settlement 15-31 days; payout biweekly. Adding a realistic 6-10 weeks to 1,000 followers, 30-day Pilot, 2-4 weeks to a first order, 31-day hold and up to 14 days to the next payout run lands at 100-150 days. The shortlist note ([[research/shortlist]]) also flagged the entry threshold itself (1K vs 5K) as unverified; the official Seller University page remains unfetched, so Gate 1 could be higher.

### D9. Average commission 13.02%, open plans 10-15%, targeted 18-25%+, beauty up to 30% (R)

- Verdict: **weakened**; direction of travel negative.
- Evidence: all rates come from agency/vendor blogs. The referral fee rose from 6% to 8% on 2026-08-04 with 9.5-11% in some beauty and electronics sub-categories ([[research/lenses/commerce-arbitrage]] lists this as V across three 2026 sources). Sellers' effective cost of sale was already 26.6-33% of GMV per the dossier; the fee rise adds 2-5 points onto the same P&L that funds affiliate commissions and samples. The dossier says "no published evidence yet of open-plan rates falling"; there is equally no evidence of them holding, and the only lever a squeezed seller controls is the commission rate. no data found on post-August open-plan rate trend.

### D10. BFCM 2025: ~$500M in 4 days, ~10M affiliate shoppable videos over the holidays (V, TikTok Newsroom)

- Verdict: **holds** as an aggregate; **weakened** as support for a nano affiliate.
- Evidence: platform newsroom figure (not fetched). $500M over 4 days is 3.3% of annual GMV, i.e. seasonal, not structural. The 10M-video figure is the saturation signal, not the demand signal: it is the supply of competing content the operator's clips must beat in the exact window the dossier tells the operator to target.

### D11. Sub-$40 kitchen/cleaning/beauty is the right lane; Beauty & Personal Care is the #1 category (V for ranking, dossier inference for the rest)

- Verdict: **weakened**.
- Evidence: category rank is Momentum Works (unfetched). The inference ignores that beauty is (a) the sub-category with the highest referral-fee tier (9.5-11%), so sellers there are most squeezed; (b) where efficacy claims attract Creator Health Rating penalties and FTC exposure, per the dossier's own risk table; (c) where the dossier's flood of AI-avatar content and agency-managed creators concentrates. Kitchen/cleaning under $40 has the same low-return advantage with less claim risk. no data found on category-level return rates or on category-level affiliate counts.

### D12. Sellers are hungry for affiliates because more than half of stores had zero sales (dossier inference)

- Verdict: **weakened**.
- Evidence: zero-sales stores are the ones that cannot fund samples or commissions; the paying "who" is the >2,000 stores above $1M GMV plus a middle tier, and those work with agencies and targeted plans. The sample quota (5 active samples under $5K monthly GMV) is rationing from the creator side, which is the platform's own signal that sample demand exceeds supply, i.e. more creators want products than sellers will give away. That is the opposite of "hungry sellers".

### D13. Human hands-on demos are less crowded because AI-avatar content is being pushed out by policy (dossier inference)

- Verdict: **weakened**.
- Evidence: [[research/saturated-overhyped]] (section 12) confirms the policy direction but grades the "70/30 human/AI" claim tier C (vendor). The pushed-out AI-avatar operators do not leave the market; they switch to exactly this format, because the AI scripting/scoring stack is sold off-the-shelf to everyone. The candidate's AI layer (Claude scripts, n8n scoring, EchoTik exports) gives zero moat; the differentiator is on-camera performance, a skill the dossier rates "new, 4-8 weeks to acceptable". AI has commoditized the part of this job that was ever a service; what is left is being a creator.

### D14. Who pays: sellers pay affiliates per order (V)

- Verdict: **holds**, but the pool is smaller than the dossier implies.
- Evidence (I): see D4. There is no platform-funded payout for Shop content; every dollar comes from seller margin already carrying 8-11% referral fees, samples and returns. The dossier's line "TikTok funded holiday incentives" refers to shopper-facing credits and seller deals, not creator payments.

### D15. ~2M US creators earn meaningful income on TikTok (R, ValueAdd VC)

- Verdict: **weakened**.
- Evidence: undefined "meaningful"; inconsistent with ~945K US creators generating any affiliate sale unless most of the 2M earn from Creator Rewards/brand deals rather than Shop. Not usable as demand evidence for this candidate.

### D16. Declining search interest / negative post-mortems / creator earnings breakdowns

- Verdict: **no data found** (Google Trends, Reddit, YouTube, Business Insider all blocked; search budget exhausted). The dossier itself recorded "no data found" on named nano/micro affiliates in kitchen/cleaning with published earnings. Two research passes, zero first-person earnings reports: that absence is itself weak evidence that the well-publicized wins are rare.

### D17. Platform crackdowns (CHR, July 2026 reset, AIGC labels, LIVE AI-voice ban)

- Verdict: **holds** as a risk the dossier already prices in.
- Evidence: consistent across the dossier, [[research/lenses/shortform-social]] and [[research/lenses/commerce-arbitrage]] (the two lens notes disagree on the LIVE ban effective date, 2026-05-23 vs 2026-07-31; unresolved). Relevant to demand only in that CHR gating raises the cost of every claim error and late sample post, which lowers the effective payout for a beginner.

## What would change the verdict

- A fetched Momentum Works report or TikTok newsroom page with 2026 H1 US GMV (would move D1 to holds and test "still growing").
- Any dataset with a median or distribution of US affiliate monthly commission (would settle D4).
- Three or more first-person 2026 earnings breakdowns from sub-10K-follower kitchen/cleaning affiliates (Reddit r/TikTokShopAffiliate, YouTube) showing $200+/month within six months.
- Evidence on open-plan commission rates before vs after 2026-08-04 (Kalodata/EchoTik category averages).
- The official Seller University affiliate eligibility page confirming 1,000 followers and the Pilot Program terms.
